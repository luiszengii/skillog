# Skillog illustration style DNA

## Core direction

Pure white, sparse, hand-drawn, slightly strange, product-sketch-like, and easy to understand at a glance. It should look like a designer quickly explaining an idea on white paper, not a polished commercial cartoon or formal infographic.

## Line looseness

Line looseness is controlled by the prompt, independently from character identity.

Default to **standard slight looseness**:

- thin black pen lines with gentle wobble;
- modest pressure variation;
- occasional subtle retracing, tiny overshoot, or incomplete geometric perfection;
- small natural asymmetries;
- clear silhouette and readable action despite the looseness.

If the user asks for cleaner lines, reduce retracing and wobble while retaining a hand-drawn feel. If the user asks for rougher lines, increase wobble and natural corrections modestly, but never distort the robot's proportions, component placement, or silhouette. Do not use heavy scribbling, fuzzy charcoal, messy hatching, or broken anatomy.

## Space and composition

- Use a pure white background with no texture, gray wash, shadow, gradient, or scene backdrop.
- Keep the main action around 40%-60% of the canvas and preserve at least 35% quiet white space.
- One image communicates one core action, relationship, state, or metaphor.
- Use one dominant scene rather than grids, framed cards, formal nodes, or dense diagrams unless the user explicitly requests a character sheet or comic.

## Color

- Black: all line work, face screen, limbs, structure, and primary handwritten marks.
- White: background and robot shell.
- Violet-blue: the robot's exact `S` and, only when useful, one tiny functional accent.
- Optional semantic annotations: orange for a main path, red for a problem or warning, blue for secondary system feedback. Use sparingly and never recolor the `S`.

## Avoid

No 3D rendering, vector-perfect curves, glossy surfaces, commercial mascot polish, children's illustration, sticker aesthetic, detailed sci-fi machinery, technology UI, paper texture, complex backgrounds, large color fills, dense copy, or PPT/course-slide appearance.
