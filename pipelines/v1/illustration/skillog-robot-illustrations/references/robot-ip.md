# Skillog robot identity

## Role and personality

The Skillog robot is a serious little system operator: calm, attentive, gently awkward, and capable. It participates in the work rather than decorating the corner. Humor comes from doing a slightly absurd task with complete sincerity. Avoid babyish cuteness, exaggerated facial acting, and superhero confidence.

## Immutable construction

Every appearance must preserve these identity anchors from `../assets/skillog-robot-reference.png`:

- One integrated white vertical rounded-rectangle capsule body, about 1.7 times as tall as wide. There is no separate head, torso, or neck.
- One horizontal black rounded-rectangle face screen in the upper front.
- Exactly two small white vertical pill-shaped eyes inside the screen. No mouth, nose, eyebrows, cheeks, or extra facial marks.
- One thin centered antenna ending in one white outlined circular ball.
- One simple white oval ear pad on each side.
- One large violet-blue capital `S` centered on the lower front. It appears only when the front surface is visible and never on the rear shell.
- Thin flexible black line arms with solid black dot hands and no fingers.
- Thin black line legs with small flat black oval feet.
- A completely plain white rear body shell: no hatch, panel, port, seam, logo, symbol, or decorative element.

Side views show the capsule depth, one dominant ear pad, the edge of the front screen, and at most the front edge of the `S`. Do not invent a side face or side logo.

## Expression system

Express emotion without adding facial anatomy. Use:

- eye spacing or slight eye tilt inside the screen;
- antenna angle;
- body lean;
- arm gesture and leg stance;
- at most two or three tiny black motion or emotion marks.

Useful states include attentive, thinking, small success, confused, worried, overwhelmed, careful, and determined. Even during strong action, keep the character recognizable and deadpan.

## Pose and prop discipline

The robot should physically carry the concept: sorting cards, pulling a cable, lifting a box, inspecting a result, opening a path, connecting two parts, or operating a simple mechanism. Prefer one or two props. Props must not hide the face screen, antenna, or most of the body silhouette unless the concept specifically requires strain or obstruction.
